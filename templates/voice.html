Purpose

Why Voice Detection
for Parkinson's?

A non-invasive screening approach grounded in acoustic neuroscience and validated clinical research.

Early Neurological Signal

Parkinson's Disease affects motor control pathways years before tremors or rigidity become clinically apparent. The laryngeal muscles are among the earliest structures to show measurable deterioration. Acoustic biomarkers such as jitter, shimmer, and harmonic-to-noise ratio capture these subtle changes at a stage when conventional examination often yields inconclusive results.

Non-Invasive and Immediate

Conventional Parkinson's assessment typically requires neurological examinations, dopamine transporter imaging, or extended clinical observation. This system requires only a brief sustained phonation, typically three to four seconds. No physical examination, no imaging equipment, and no hospital visit are necessary. The MDVP feature extraction pipeline processes 22 acoustic parameters from a single voice sample automatically.

Clinically Validated Features

This model was trained on the UCI Parkinson's Dataset, comprising 195 voice recordings from 31 subjects. The feature set includes RPDE, DFA, and PPE, derived from nonlinear dynamical analysis and validated in peer-reviewed clinical literature as reliable indicators of neurodegeneration. The model operates on a scientifically established foundation, not on statistical correlation alone.
